Methodology

  • Format. Every pair of strategies plays 8 games. Sides are balanced — each strategy starts half its games and sits in each physical seat half the time — so first-move bias cancels out. Deck luck is controlled by duplicate deals: the pairing plays 4 fixed shuffles twice each, with the entrants swapped and the deck order, starting seat and in-game randomness held identical, so each deal is worth the same to both sides. Deal sets are drawn independently per pairing. It is a modest effect — roughly a 10% cut in the variance of a head-to-head estimate, since the two halves diverge as soon as the strategies choose differently and only the opening hands are truly shared.
  • Outcome. Lowest hand sum wins (Cambio caller takes a +5 penalty if it isn't lowest). Equal sums are broken in favour of the hand holding more cards; a genuine tie (equal sum and equal card count) counts as half a win to each side. Only win/loss/tie is recorded; margin is ignored.
  • Rating. A Bradley-Terry model is fit to all pairwise results at once (Hunter 2004 MM iteration), then mapped to an Elo-style scale via rating = anchor + 400·log10(strength ratio). Random is fixed at 1500 as the calibration floor; every other rating reads as points above coin-flip play. A small symmetric prior keeps every rating finite. A 400-point gap ≈ a 10:1 expected win ratio.
  • Confidence intervals. The 95% bands come from a nonparametric pairwise bootstrap: each of 2,000 replicates resamples every pairing's recorded deal pairs with replacement, refits Bradley-Terry, and re-anchors. The band is the percentile spread of each rating across replicates — overlapping bands mean the gap isn't resolved at this game count. Random sits exactly at the 1500 anchor in every replicate, so its band is a degenerate point.
